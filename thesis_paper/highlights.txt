Elsevier Highlights -- submitted as a separate file, not part of the PDF.
Three to five bullets, each at most 85 characters including spaces.

FINAL SET (checked by tests/test_paper_highlights.py, not by hand):

- Agentic KGQA framework pairs every answer with the triples that support it
- Matches or beats four baselines at roughly half the language-model calls
- Removing the planner improves accuracy on the shallower benchmark, cutting tokens 31%
- Three of four agentic components show no detectable accuracy effect
- The echo attractor: a true entity one hop away that consensus cannot detect

Rationale for this set, if it needs defending to a co-author:

The order runs system, performance, attribution, attribution, mechanism.
Bullets 3 and 4 are the paper's real contribution and get two of the five
slots. Bullet 5 is the one other people are most likely to cite, because
it is a claim about evaluation methodology rather than about this system.

Deliberately NOT included: any claim that the verification layer raises
accuracy. It does not (Section 6), and a highlight promising it would be
contradicted by the paper it introduces.

Bullet 3 carries "on the shallower benchmark" because the planner result
is not a both-benchmark result: removing the planner improves WebQSP
(+0.083 F1, p = 0.006) and costs ComplexWebQuestions (-0.047 F1,
p = 0.088), and the 31% token cut is the WebQSP figure too -- CWQ is
21%. An earlier version of this bullet said "improves accuracy and cuts
tokens by 31%" with no scope, which fails the same standard the previous
paragraph applies: it would have been contradicted by the paper it
introduces, in a document an editor reads before the abstract. At 85
characters it is exactly at the limit, so anything added must displace
something, not extend it.

Also dropped: the measured label-defect rates for the two benchmarks.
True and interesting, but it needs both the flagged and the confirmed
count to be honest, and that does not fit in 85 characters.
