FINAL SET (checked by tests/test_paper_highlights.py, not by hand):

- An agentic KGQA system taken apart: one component removed at a time, paired tests
- The margin over the agentic baseline comes entirely from budget truncation
- Removing the planner improves accuracy on the shallower benchmark, cutting tokens 31%
- Backtracking, model scoring and claim checking show no effect at the power available
- The echo attractor: a true entity one hop away that consensus repair cannot detect

Rationale for this set, if it needs defending to a co-author:

Reframed in September 2026 with the manuscript. The order runs
instrument, budget finding, attribution, attribution, mechanism. Bullets
2-4 are the paper's contribution and get three of the five slots.
Bullet 5 is the one other people are most likely to cite, because it is
a claim about evaluation methodology rather than about this system.

Deliberately NOT included: any claim that the verification layer raises
accuracy (it does not, Section 6), and "matches or beats four baselines
at roughly half the calls", which an earlier set carried. Half the calls
is true against the agentic baseline only; AGR uses six to nine times
the calls of the three single-call controls, and a reviewer read the
bullet as claiming otherwise.

Bullet 3 carries "on the shallower benchmark" because the planner result
is not a both-benchmark result: removing the planner improves WebQSP
(+0.083 F1, p = 0.006) and costs ComplexWebQuestions (-0.047 F1,
p = 0.088), and the 31% token cut is the WebQSP figure too -- CWQ is
21%. At 85 characters it is exactly at the limit, so anything added must
displace something, not extend it.

Bullet 4 says "at the power available" rather than "no effect", because
Section 6.3 shows the tests could not have detected a difference below
4-5.5 accuracy points.

Also dropped: the measured label-defect rates for the two benchmarks.
True and interesting, but it needs both the flagged and the confirmed
count to be honest, and that does not fit in 85 characters.
