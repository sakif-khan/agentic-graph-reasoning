% AT MOST 200 WORDS -- OUR line, not a quoted requirement. An earlier
% version of this comment attributed the figure to the publisher, which

% asks only for "a concise and factual abstract" and sets no word limit

% 200 is house style, and the draft sat at 215 words until something
% counted it. tests/test_paper_abstract.py counts it now.
%
% Leads with the system and closes on the attribution result, which is
% what the paper actually establishes. It must NOT promise that
% verification raises accuracy -- Section 6 reports the null, and an
% abstract that oversells it turns that section into a retraction.
Multi-hop question answering over knowledge graphs composes facts across
several edges, so systems that retrieve once before reasoning cannot
know what the second hop will need. Agentic approaches interleave
retrieval with reasoning, but none checks that what the final answer
asserts is supported by what the agent retrieved. We present
Agentic Graph Reasoning (AGR), an explicit state machine over a
constrained graph tool API whose structural verification layer
decomposes each draft answer into atomic claims and checks them against
the traversed triples before emission, attaching those that match as
evidence. On $400$ certified questions from each of WebQSP and
ComplexWebQuestions over a Freebase-derived environment, AGR reaches
Hits@1 of $0.755$ and $0.522$ against four baselines sharing one frozen
backbone and call budget, using roughly half the language-model calls of
the strongest. A component-level ablation with paired significance
testing finds that removing the planner \emph{improves} accuracy on the
shallower benchmark while cutting tokens by $31\%$, and that three of
four components --- including claim verification --- show no detectable
accuracy effect. A hand-read census of all $259$ residual failures
identifies the \emph{echo attractor}: a true, grounded entity one hop
from the answer that passes every grounding check and that
consensus-based evaluation cannot detect.
\end{abstract}

Code, prompts, per-question run records, and the analysis scripts that
produce every figure and table in this paper are available at
\url{https://github.com/sakif-khan/agentic-graph-reasoning} under the MIT

question sets are the public WebQSP~\cite{yih2016value} and
ComplexWebQuestions~\cite{talmor2018web} releases.
