\begin{abstract}
% AT MOST 200 WORDS -- OUR line, not a quoted requirement (the KBS guide
% is unfetchable; a search-engine summary of it says 250). Counted by
% tests/test_paper_abstract.py.
%
% Reframed in September 2026: leads with the attribution question, names
% the system as the instrument, and gives the findings in the order the
% paper reports them (aggregate margin located, planner asymmetry, three
% nulls at stated power, census). It must NOT promise that verification
% raises accuracy -- Section 6 reports the null.
Agentic systems for multi-hop question answering over knowledge graphs
assemble decomposition, guided search, backtracking, and
self-correction, and the literature reports them as wholes. Which
component produces a reported gain is rarely established. We build an
agentic system, AGR, whose four components can each be removed by
construction, run it against four baselines on one frozen backbone under
one call budget, and take it apart. On $400$ questions from each of
WebQSP and ComplexWebQuestions, AGR leads the agentic baseline by $9.5$
and $8.7$ Hits@1 points. The entire margin comes from questions on which
the shared budget truncates the baseline; where the baseline finishes,
it is ahead. Removing the planner improves WebQSP F1 by $0.083$ ($p =
0.006$) and cuts tokens by $31\%$. The other three components, a
claim-verification layer among them, show no effect at the power
available, and we report the smallest effect each test could have
detected. A hand-read census of the residual failures finds label
defects in $5.5\%$ and $4.8\%$ of the samples and names the \emph{echo
attractor}: a true, grounded entity one hop from the answer on which
systems sharing a graph and a backbone converge, so that
consensus-based label repair ratifies it.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability}
% Verified public: the URL returns HTTP 200 to an unauthenticated
% request. Re-check if the repository is ever made private, because an
% editor does check this and a reviewer resents finding a 404.
Code, prompts, per-question run records, and the analysis scripts that
produce every figure and table in this paper are available at
\url{https://github.com/sakif-khan/agentic-graph-reasoning} under the
MIT licence. The question sets are the public WebQSP~\cite{yih2016value}
and ComplexWebQuestions~\cite{talmor2018web} releases, and both they
and the Freebase-derived~\cite{bollacker2008freebase} subgraphs arrive
through the public Reasoning-on-Graphs
distribution~\cite{luo2024reasoning} (\textsf{rmanluo/RoG-webqsp} and
\textsf{rmanluo/RoG-cwq}), used unmodified. The knowledge environment
is rebuilt from that distribution by the scripts in the repository
rather than redistributed.
