Two annotation tasks in this thesis produced hand labels: the gold-quality
adjudication of \Cref{sec:gold-quality}, and the failure census of
\Cref{ch:erroranalysis}. This appendix gives the instructions and the label
schema both were carried out against, so that the categories the census reports
can be read as definitions rather than as summary words.

\section{Judge Instructions}\label{app:judge-rules}

The semantic-support judge of \Cref{sec:judge} is shown three things: the
question, \emph{one} entity the system asserted as an answer to it, and up to
three knowledge-graph paths connecting the question's topic entity to that
entity. It decides one thing: \emph{do these paths express the relationship the
question asks about, such that this entity is supported as an answer?} Mere
connectedness is not support, and the prompt says so in those words.

The unit of judgement is therefore an \emph{entity together with its paths}, not
a claim and not a set of triples. That distinction is what the following rules
are about, and it is why several of them have no analogue in the claim-level
structural check of \Cref{sec:structural-check}: that check asks whether an edge
exists, whereas this one asks whether the edges that exist mean what the question
asked. The sampling frame is built from asserted entities, so an abstention never
reaches this task at all.

Eight rules constrain the judgement. Each was forced by a case met during
calibration, and the guideline was fixed \emph{before} the blind annotation
began. That order is worth stating explicitly, because the alternative reading
would invalidate the statistic it produced: a guideline revised in response to
the disagreements it is later measured on records how well two passes were
reconciled, not whether they agreed. The sequence was guideline first, then the
blind pass of \Cref{sec:judge} --- one hundred items labelled without opening
the judge's output or the answer key --- and $\kappa = 0.6995$ against a
pre-registered bar of $0.70$ is what that pass returned. The outcome is reported
there rather than here.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Judge the asked relationship, under the graph's vocabulary.} The
  test is whether the path relations express what the question asked, as the
  graph is able to express it --- not whether a better-designed schema would
  have said it more directly. A path that is merely \emph{about} the right
  entities does not qualify.
\item \textbf{A mediator-bridged pair of edges is one relation.} Freebase
  routes many binary facts through an unnamed compound-value node, so the path
  renders as two hops. It is judged as the single relationship it encodes, on
  the same contract the tool layer uses (\Cref{sec:tool-adjacency}).
\item \textbf{Any one supporting path is enough.} Up to three are shown. If one
  of them expresses the asked relationship, the entity is supported; the others
  need not, and a weak path alongside a good one is not evidence against.
\item \textbf{Judge relations by role, not by type.} A relation that is generic
  in most contexts counts when it is itself the thing asked about --- a
  \textsf{nationality} edge is generic filler for a question about films and is
  the entire answer for a question about citizenship.
\item \textbf{For conjunctions, every asked relationship must be covered by some
  specific path, jointly rather than in one chain.} A question imposing two
  constraints is supported when the paths together establish both, even if no
  single path establishes both at once.
\item \textbf{Judge each entity independently of answer-set completeness.}
  Whether the system found the \emph{other} correct answers is recall, and it is
  measured by F1 elsewhere. This task asks only whether \emph{this} assertion is
  grounded.
\item \textbf{Ignore superlative and temporal filters the graph cannot express.}
  Where a question asks for the first, largest, or most recent of something and
  the environment encodes no ordinals or date literals
  (\Cref{sec:unanswerable}), judge only the base relationship. Penalising the
  path for a filter the environment cannot represent would measure the
  environment, not the assertion.
\item \textbf{A path expressing only the question's identifying descriptor,
  rather than its actual ask, is unsupported.} ``The 33rd president who led the
  nation during the war'' identifies its subject twice and asks for it once; a
  path that only re-establishes the description has answered nothing.
\end{enumerate}

Rule 8 names the \emph{composition-echo} case; \Cref{sec:judge} draws out why it
matters that the failure census arrives at the same mechanism independently.

\section{Failure Census Label Schema}\label{app:census-schema}

Every packet in the census receives one \textsf{category}, an optional
\textsf{subtype}, and a one-sentence note. The note pinpoints where the
trajectory \emph{first} left the correct path --- plan, then explored relations,
then backtracks, then verifier --- rather than the last symptom. That ordering
is the whole discipline of the exercise: a wrong answer usually has several
things wrong with it by the end, and only the first one is a cause.

\subsection*{Convention for the verifier categories}

The verifier's positive class is \emph{claim is supported}. A false negative is
therefore a claim wrongly \emph{rejected}, and a false positive is a claim
wrongly \emph{accepted}. This is the standard convention and the one the census
labels use throughout. It is stated explicitly because the gloss in the working
reference originally paired the two names in reverse, and one Stage~A label was
filed against the reversed reading before the error was found. Stage~D's labels
followed the standard convention throughout. That one label was corrected;
\Cref{sec:census-corrections} records it alongside the one other relabelling,
which was made for an unrelated reason.

\subsection*{Gold-defect subtypes}

Seventeen gold defects survive into the census itself --- $3$ on WebQSP and $14$
on CWQ, filed as \textsf{gold\_noise} ($7$) or \textsf{ambiguous\_question}
($10$) and reported as such in \Cref{sec:benchmark-defects}. They are not
subtyped there, because the census met each one incidentally while reading a
failure, whereas the adjudication pass of \Cref{sec:gold-quality} met roughly
$148$ flagged rows and made exactly this judgement at volume. Subtyping a defect
class is only worth doing where the boundaries were drawn against many cases, so
the adjudication pass supplies the reference list and the census rows are counted
against its categories rather than given a vocabulary of their own. \Cref{tab:golddefect-subtypes} gives them with the counts they
were derived from.

\Cref{tab:labelfiles} settles it.

After any relabelling, three scripts must run in order:
\textsf{pull\_noplanner\_categories.py}, then \textsf{synthesize\_census.py},
then \textsf{build\_thesis\_numbers.py}. Skipping the last leaves the thesis
quoting the previous histogram.

\subsection*{Corrections made after the first labelling pass}

Three corrections were made after the initial pass --- one to the working
reference and two to labels --- and are recorded here rather than silently
absorbed.

\begin{itemize}
\item The false-positive and false-negative gloss in the working reference
  paired the two names in reverse of the standard convention. It was corrected,
  and the convention is now stated explicitly above. Stage~D's labels had
  followed the standard convention throughout and none was changed on this
  account; one Stage~A label had not, and is the second item below.
\item \textsf{WebQTrn-1294}, in the Stage~D census, moved from
  \textsf{verifier\_fn} to \textsf{answer\_selection}. This one is unrelated to
  the convention: the verifier had been correct, and the error was upstream, in
  the answerer.
\item \textsf{WebQTrn-1597}, in the Stage~A planner-discordance census, moved
  from \textsf{verifier\_fn} to \textsf{verifier\_fp}, the original label having
  used the reversed convention. This is the only label the gloss error reached.
\end{itemize}

The histogram was regenerated afterwards. Two CWQ hedge rows moved between
categories, one in each census; both stayed within \textsf{hedge}, so every
total is unchanged.
