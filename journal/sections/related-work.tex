\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Three lines of work bear on question answering over knowledge graphs:
what retrieval fixed before reasoning can reach, what interleaving
retrieval with reasoning buys, and what checking an answer against
evidence adds. None establishes which component of an agentic system is
responsible for its behaviour, and that gap is the one this paper
addresses.

Retrieval-augmented generation~\cite{lewis2020retrieval} embeds the
question, retrieves similar passages, and answers from them. Where the
answer must be composed across passages the ranking signal measures the
wrong thing, because the passage carrying the intermediate entity
resembles neither the question nor anything the system yet knows to look
for; surveys of the graph--language-model intersection identify this as
the central limitation of flat retrieval~\cite{li2023survey}. Knowledge
graphs make the missing structure explicit, storing facts as typed edges
between identified entities so that composition becomes a traversal.
GraphRAG~\cite{edge2024local} builds a graph over a corpus and retrieves
structured context in place of passages, and a second family keeps
retrieval static but learns it, scoring candidate subgraphs with a graph
neural network~\cite{mavromatis2024gnnrag} or retrieving a bounded
subgraph with a light-weight scorer~\cite{li2025subgraphrag}. All of
them retrieve once, before reasoning, so a fixed-radius neighbourhood is
either too small to contain the answer or so large that the relevant
edges are lost among thousands of irrelevant ones.

Interleaving retrieval with reasoning removes that constraint.
Think-on-Graph~\cite{sun2024think} established the pattern for knowledge
graphs with model-guided beam search over relations and entities, and
its successor adds document retrieval at each step~\cite{ma2024tog2}.
Plan-on-Graph~\cite{chen2024plan} added decomposition into
sub-objectives and reflection-driven backtracking to that beam search;
AGR's planner and backtracker are those two mechanisms re-hosted in a
program-orchestrated loop, its verification layer is the addition, and
\Cref{sec:limits-comparison} records that Plan-on-Graph itself is not
among the systems run here. Reasoning-on-Graphs
(RoG)~\cite{luo2024reasoning} takes a different route, generating
grounded relation paths as explicit plans with a fine-tuned model and
traversing them, so answers are faithful by construction;
graph-constrained reasoning~\cite{luo2025gcr} reaches the same guarantee
by constraining decoding to paths in the graph. RoG is also the surveyed
system whose data distribution this paper shares, since the environment
of \Cref{sec:environment} is built from its published subgraphs and
\Cref{sec:rog} compares the two directly. KBQA-o1~\cite{luo2025kbqao1}
is closest to AGR's search strategy and the most important to
distinguish from it: it performs agentic knowledge base question
answering with full Monte Carlo Tree Search wrapping a
ReAct-style~\cite{yao2023react} agent that generates logical forms
against Freebase. AGR does none of this. Its search is guided best-first
with a bounded beam and backtracking, and it carries neither rollouts
nor value backpropagation.

A parallel line checks generated content after the fact.
Chain-of-Verification~\cite{dhuliawala2024chain} has a model draft a
response, plan verification questions, answer those independently, and
regenerate --- checking against the model's own knowledge rather than an
external symbolic source, whose limits are
documented~\cite{huang2024selfcorrect}. A second group critiques the
\emph{retrieval}, training the model to emit reflection tokens judging
whether a passage supports its output~\cite{asai2024selfrag} or grading
retrieved documents and triggering a fallback search when they are
poor~\cite{yan2024crag}, and FActScore~\cite{min2023factscore}
decomposes generations into atomic facts as a metric rather than a
control step. The layer of \Cref{sec:verification} sits between these
lines: it decomposes a draft into claims and checks them against an
external source, but the source is the set of triples the agent itself
traversed and the check runs before the answer is emitted. The
path-faithful systems~\cite{luo2024reasoning,luo2025gcr} make the
question moot by never generating a claim the graph did not produce. To
our knowledge no graph-navigating agent that generates its answer freely
checks that answer's claims against its own traversal before emission,
and whether that check earns its place is what \Cref{sec:attribution}
measures.

The gap that motivates this paper is therefore methodological. Agentic
systems are assemblies of decomposition, scoring, backtracking, and
self-correction, and the literature reports them as wholes, so a
published gain over a static baseline rarely establishes which mechanism
produced it, whether some contribute nothing, or how much is the budget
regime the comparison ran under. The exception is instructive: KBQA-o1's
own ablation removes MCTS while leaving every other component intact,
and GrailQA F1 drops from $78.5$ to $48.5$~\cite{luo2025kbqao1} --- a
thirty-point attribution to a single mechanism, obtained precisely
because the authors ablated rather than reporting the assembly whole.
\Cref{sec:attribution} applies the same instrument to AGR, with paired
tests and a stated minimum detectable effect, and reports the components
for which it detects nothing at the same length as the one for which it
does. \Cref{sec:margin} adds the second source of a reported gain, the
budget, which an ablation cannot see.
