\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Three lines of work bear on question answering over knowledge graphs:
what retrieval fixed before reasoning can reach, what interleaving
retrieval with reasoning buys, and what checking an answer against
evidence adds. None establishes which component of an agentic system is
responsible for its behaviour, and that gap is the one this paper
addresses.

Retrieval-augmented generation~\cite{lewis2020retrieval} embeds the
question, retrieves similar passages, and answers from them. Where the
answer must be composed across passages the ranking signal measures the
wrong thing, because the passage carrying the intermediate entity
resembles neither the question nor anything the system yet knows to look
for; surveys of the graph--language-model intersection identify this as
the central limitation of flat retrieval~\cite{li2023survey}. Knowledge
graphs make the missing structure explicit, so composition becomes a
traversal: GraphRAG~\cite{edge2024local} retrieves structured context in
place of passages, and a second family keeps retrieval static but learns
it, scoring candidate subgraphs with a graph neural
network~\cite{mavromatis2024gnnrag} or retrieving a bounded subgraph
with a light-weight scorer~\cite{li2025subgraphrag}. All of them
retrieve once, before reasoning, so a fixed-radius neighbourhood is
either too small to contain the answer or so large that the relevant
edges are lost among thousands of irrelevant ones.

Interleaving retrieval with reasoning removes that constraint.
Think-on-Graph~\cite{sun2024think} established the pattern with
model-guided beam search over relations and entities, and its successor
adds document retrieval at each step~\cite{ma2024tog2}.
Plan-on-Graph~\cite{chen2024plan} added decomposition into
sub-objectives and reflection-driven backtracking; AGR's planner and
backtracker are those two mechanisms re-hosted in a program-orchestrated
loop, its verification layer is the addition, and
\Cref{sec:limits-comparison} records that Plan-on-Graph itself is not
among the systems run here. Reasoning-on-Graphs
(RoG)~\cite{luo2024reasoning} instead generates grounded relation paths
as explicit plans with a fine-tuned model and traverses them, so answers
are faithful by construction, as does graph-constrained
reasoning~\cite{luo2025gcr}; RoG is also the system whose published
subgraphs this paper's environment is built from
(\Cref{sec:environment}), and \ref{sec:rog} compares the two directly.
KBQA-o1~\cite{luo2025kbqao1} is closest to AGR's search strategy,
performing agentic knowledge base question answering with full Monte
Carlo Tree Search over a ReAct-style~\cite{yao2023react} agent. AGR does
none of this: its search is guided best-first with a bounded beam and
backtracking, carrying neither rollouts nor value backpropagation.

A parallel line checks generated content after the fact.
Chain-of-Verification~\cite{dhuliawala2024chain} has a model draft a
response, plan verification questions, answer those independently and
regenerate, checking against the model's own knowledge rather than an
external symbolic source, whose limits are
documented~\cite{huang2024selfcorrect}. A second group critiques the
\emph{retrieval}, emitting reflection tokens that judge whether a
passage supports the output~\cite{asai2024selfrag} or grading retrieved
documents and triggering a fallback search~\cite{yan2024crag}, while
FActScore~\cite{min2023factscore} decomposes generations into atomic
facts as a metric rather than a control step. The layer of
\Cref{sec:verification} sits between these lines: it decomposes a draft
into claims and checks them against an external source, but the source
is the set of triples the agent itself traversed and the check runs
before the answer is emitted. The path-faithful
systems~\cite{luo2024reasoning,luo2025gcr} make the question moot by
never generating a claim the graph did not produce. To our knowledge no
graph-navigating agent that generates its answer freely checks that
answer's claims against its own traversal before emission, and whether
that check earns its place is what \Cref{sec:attribution} measures.

The gap that motivates this paper is therefore methodological. Agentic
systems are assemblies, and the literature reports them as wholes, so a
published gain over a static baseline rarely establishes which mechanism
produced it, whether some contribute nothing, or how much is the budget
regime the comparison ran under. The exception is instructive: KBQA-o1's
own ablation removes MCTS while leaving every other component intact,
and GrailQA F1 drops from $78.5$ to $48.5$~\cite{luo2025kbqao1} --- a
thirty-point attribution to a single mechanism, obtained precisely
because the authors ablated. \Cref{sec:attribution} applies the same
instrument to AGR, and \Cref{sec:margin} adds the second source of a
reported gain, the budget, which an ablation cannot see.
